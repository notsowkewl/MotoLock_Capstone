Component Breakdown

1. **Waterproof Enclosure (The Black Box):** Instead of exposed breadboards, the entire circuit is housed in a rugged, IP67-rated ABS plastic project box. This protects the sensitive electronics from the elements (rain, dust, and engine heat).  
2. **The Internal PCB (Green Board):** All components—the ESP32 microcontroller, the SIM7600 LTE module, the LM2596 buck converter, and the GPS module—are permanently soldered onto a custom Printed Circuit Board (PCB) or a standard perfboard. This completely eliminates the risk of jumper wires vibrating loose while riding.  
3. **The Engine Control Relay (Blue Component):** This is the 1-Channel 5V Relay. Notice it is safely contained inside the box, isolated from weather. The heavy-duty wires that intercept the motorcycle's starter circuit connect directly to its screw terminals.  
4. **Cable Glands (The Wire Exits):** Where the wires exit the black box to connect to the motorcycle's battery and ignition, they pass through waterproof cable glands (the circular grommets on the side). This ensures water cannot travel down the wires and into the electronics.  
5. **Wire Loom Tubing (The Black Hoses):** The actual copper wires are bundled inside a black, ribbed automotive wire loom (or thick heat-shrink tubing). This prevents the motorcycle's hot engine or sharp metal frame from slicing into the wires over time, strictly adhering to automotive safety standards.

2\. How to Install using T-Taps (Zero Damage)  
To borrow a motorcycle for your defense without destroying its wiring, you will use T-Tap Connectors. A T-Tap simply bites into the insulation of a wire to steal a signal, and can be unclipped later.

1. Power (12V): Locate the motorcycle's ACC (Accessory) wire coming from the ignition key switch. Clamp a T-Tap onto it. Plug your MotoLock's LM2596 positive input into this T-Tap. (The system now turns on when the key is turned).  
2. Ground (GND): Clamp a T-Tap onto a ground wire, or bolt a wire to the motorcycle's metal frame. Connect this to your LM2596 negative input.  
3. The Relay Intercept:  
   * Locate the thin wire coming from the motorcycle's Brake Lever Switch (for scooters) or Starter Button (for manual bikes).  
   * Note: This is the only wire you must actually cut.  
   * Connect the side coming from the button to the COM port of your Relay.  
   * Connect the side going down into the motorcycle to the NO (Normally Open) port of your Relay.  
   * (When you return the motorcycle, just twist those two cut ends back together and wrap them in electrical tape—good as new\!)

3\. Capstone Defense: Answering the Professor's GPS & Scalability Concern  
Your professor is absolutely correct: Continuous live tracking (updating every second) is incredibly expensive. It drains mobile data (SIM load) rapidly and costs thousands of dollars in cloud server database storage.

Here is exactly how you answer this concern to your panel:

*"Panelists, you are completely correct that continuous live GPS tracking is expensive and unscalable. However, the MotoLock System does NOT use continuous tracking. We use Event-Driven Architecture."*

The Strategy: Event-Driven GPS  
The NEO-M8N GPS module on the motorcycle is passively listening to satellites, but it **does not transmit data to the internet or send SMS during normal riding.**  
It ONLY triggers a transmission (via the SIM7600 module) when a specific critical event occurs:

1. The rider fails the alcohol breath test.  
2. The manual override is forcefully activated.

**Cost Defense:** Because it only transmits data during an emergency, a standard prepaid SIM card load (e.g., 50 PHP) will last for months because it only sends 1 or 2 SMS messages ever.

Software & API Integration  
When an event triggers, the SIM7600 executes a simple **HTTP POST Request** to your Node.js backend (e.g., POST /api/alerts) containing the coordinates, or it sends a standard SMS.  
**Scalability Defense (Max Users):**  
*"Because our hardware only communicates with the backend during an emergency event, our server is not being spammed with GPS data every second. This means a standard, budget-friendly $5/month cloud server (like DigitalOcean or AWS EC2) running our Node.js/MySQL backend can easily support 10,000 to 50,000+ users. At any given moment, 99.9% of the MotoLock hardware units are completely silent and consuming zero server bandwidth."*
